## Business and thesis

HSBC earns from deposit-funded lending, transaction banking, wealth management and markets. Its international network can support sticky deposits and cross-border fees. The investment case requires durable returns above the cost of equity, disciplined costs and capital distributions without weakening solvency. The main counter-case is that today's profitability is flattered by interest rates and benign credit losses while China/Hong Kong exposure and regulatory capital requirements deserve a larger discount.

## Quarterly tests and invalidation

| KPI | Reinforcement | Analyst review / failure gate |
|---|---|---|
| RoTE and banking NII | Returns persist without unusually low provisions | RoTE below 14% for two quarters, after adjusting genuine one-offs, plus a weaker forward earnings bridge |
| CET1 and distributions | Capital rebuilt after regulatory change | CET1 below 13% or payout restriction: immediate review; this is an analyst threshold, not a claim about the regulatory minimum |
| Credit | Stable loss rates, transparent property exposure | Persistent provision escalation and impaired capital generation |
| Wealth fees and deposits | Organic fee growth and sticky funding | Deposit outflows, higher funding cost and weak fee growth together |
| Per-share economics | TNAV/EPS growth after distributions | Buybacks funded at the expense of capital resilience |

Next gates are full-year results, 2027 capital implementation and credit disclosures; verify the next issuer earnings date before trading. Current status: **intact, valuation under review**. A mild rate decline is not automatically a thesis break; credit losses and capital distribution capacity matter more than a single quarter's headline profit.
