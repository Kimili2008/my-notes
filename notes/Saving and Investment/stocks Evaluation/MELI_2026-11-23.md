## Business and operating thesis

Commerce, logistics, payments, advertising and lending reinforce one another: merchant density and fulfillment improve buyer experience; payments and credit deepen engagement; transaction data can improve underwriting and ad relevance. The thesis requires these interactions to produce rising cash earnings per share after shipping subsidies, loan losses, funding costs and reinvestment. Scale without better economics would weaken the case even if sales remain strong. Competition, currency changes, consumer credit and management's willingness to keep reinvesting can delay shareholder returns.

## Latest evidence

The [Q3 2026 shareholder letter filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1099590/000109959026000027/meli-20260930xex991.htm) reports revenue/financial income **$10.75 billion (+45% YoY)**, operating income **$676 million (−6%)**, operating margin **6.3%**, and net income **$448 million**. GMV was $23 billion and TPV $109 billion. The credit book approached $18 billion; NIMAL was 20.1% and 15–90 day NPL 7.0%. Credit cards represented 47% of the credit portfolio. Management attributes margin pressure partly to shipping/fulfillment and customer-acquisition investment; the future payback remains a hypothesis. Reported operating cash flow also includes customer-fund and lending-related movements, so OCF minus capex alone is not clean owner earnings.

## Catalysts, KPIs and falsification

| Test | Reinforces thesis | Analyst review / failure condition |
|---|---|---|
| Commerce growth | Organic, FX-neutral GMV and repeat purchasing improve | Two quarters of growth slowdown while subsidy intensity keeps rising |
| Shipping economics | Cost per shipment and contribution improve | Volume grows but shipping contribution and consolidated margin deteriorate persistently |
| Credit | Mature cohorts earn attractive loss-adjusted returns | NIMAL below 17% alongside 15–90 day NPL above 9%: immediate review |
| Profit conversion | Operating profit catches up with revenue | Operating margin below 5% for two quarters without credible incremental-return evidence |
| Funding/cash | Lending growth supported by stable funding | Dependence on expensive funding or customer-fund movements to mask weak cash generation |
| Currency/dilution | USD earnings per share compound | FX and diluted shares repeatedly offset operating gains |

These numerical gates are analyst monitoring rules, not management guidance or validated statistical cutoffs. A single investment-heavy quarter is insufficient to declare the thesis broken. Next evidence comes from holiday-quarter results, shipping productivity and loan-vintage seasoning; confirm the next earnings date from [issuer IR](https://investor.mercadolibre.com/news-and-events). Current status: **growth reinforced; margin and credit conversion under review**.
