# Portfolio Event Note | Nu Holdings Q3 2026

**13 November 2026 | 08:00 GMT | Post-earnings review**  
**Event classification:** Material thesis update. **Thesis status:** core growth and profitability **reinforced**; credit quality and new-market economics **under review**. No operating-thesis breaker has yet been met.

## Investment conclusion

Nu's Q3 release showed record **$6.3 billion gross revenue** (+37% year on year, FX-neutral), **$1.1 billion net income** (+31%) and **32% ROE**. Customer count reached **143 million**. Those results support the September thesis that the digital banking model can scale profitably. The counterweight is rising delinquency: **90+ day non-performing loans (NPLs) increased to 7.3% from 6.9% in Q2**, approaching the card's 7.5% watch threshold; 15–90 day NPLs edged up to 4.9% from 4.8%. Reported risk-adjusted net interest margin eased to **12.1% from 12.4%**. [Nu Q3 release](https://nu.com/en/newsroom/company/nu-holdings-ltd-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results).

Nu also disclosed that **Nu US launched on 10 September** and **Nu Global on 15 October**. The U.S. offering includes a 3.5% APY account and 1.5% cashback card; Nu Global is a stablecoin-based multicurrency account. Their combined efficiency-ratio impact remains within management's stated ceiling of **100 basis points in each of 2026 and 2027**. This is a geographic and product expansion beyond the Brazil/Mexico/Colombia scope modeled in the existing card. It creates option value, but unit economics, regulatory obligations and adoption quality are not yet demonstrated. Nu's U.S. services are offered through partner Lead Bank; the release does not imply Nu itself has a U.S. bank charter. [Nu Q3 release](https://nu.com/en/newsroom/company/nu-holdings-ltd-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results).

An after-hours/last-trade quote is not an executable price.

The September card's **+3/10 rating** and **$17–$18 risk-adjusted 12-month value** should be treated as stale until the Q3 credit migration and U.S./Global investment are modeled. The profit beat supports the numerator of expected value, while rising late delinquency can reduce normalized earnings and new-market spending can defer returns. A credible numerical revision needs vintage-level loss rates, cost of risk and write-offs, funding costs, Mexico bank economics, and separate U.S./Global customer acquisition cost, deposits and contribution margin. In their absence, neither the Q3 earnings headline nor the post-results share decline justifies a new target price.

## Catalysts and conditional action framework

1. **Next 1–2 quarters:** Track 15–90 and 90+ NPLs, coverage, write-offs and reported risk-adjusted NIM. If 90+ NPL breaches **7.5%** and early delinquency accelerates, reduce normalized ROE and the card's rating; if early-stage arrears stabilize and credit costs remain contained, the core thesis is reinforced.
2. **Q4 efficiency and new markets:** Test whether expenses shifted from Q3 reappear, whether 2026 efficiency still lands at 19%–19.5%, and whether U.S./Global spending stays inside the 100 bp commitment. Require evidence of engaged, funded customers rather than geography count alone.
3. **Positioning:** Re-mark NU at a live executable quote and assess its weight in the full account before any change. A measured add would require stable credit migration and credible new-market unit economics; a trim would be warranted if credit deterioration or funding cost overwhelms growth. Do not infer a trade from one day's price response.
