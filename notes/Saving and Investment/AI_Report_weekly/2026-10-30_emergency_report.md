## Investment conclusion

Amazon's 29 October Q3 release gives both sides of the written thesis sharper evidence. AWS sales grew **39% year over year to $46.0 billion** and AWS operating income reached **$17.8 billion**; consolidated operating income was **$28.1 billion**, up from $17.4 billion a year earlier. Yet trailing-12-month free cash flow deteriorated to **negative $42.4 billion**, from negative $7.6 billion at the June 2026 reference in the user's thesis card and positive $14.8 billion a year earlier. Amazon attributes the deterioration primarily to AI-related property and equipment investment and still expects about **$220 billion of 2026 cash capital expenditure**. The cloud-demand thesis strengthened, but the thesis card's central question—whether AI investment will convert into durable *per-share free cash flow*—is more pressing, not resolved. ## Expected-value implications

The September card assigned **+4/10** and a **$335–347 risk-adjusted 12-month target** from a roughly $250 reference, using a FY2027 normalized-EPS base case of $17. These valuation outputs **should not be rolled forward unchanged**. The Q3 AWS result strengthens the probability of the high-growth scenario, while the larger cash deficit and continued capex increase the probability of a longer payback or lower terminal free-cash-flow yield. There is no defensible point estimate for a revised target without modeling 2027–28 capex, energy and depreciation, AWS utilization, contract economics, and retail/advertising contribution. A strong operating quarter does not by itself turn capex into economic profit.

The card's explicit breakers are **not yet met**: AWS has not slowed below 20%, retail operating income remains positive, and AWS profit is growing. Its cash-flow breaker requires *persistent* negative FCF **and** inability to explain a recovery path. Q3 establishes a substantially larger negative FCF figure, but the recovery-path test remains open. The correct status is **under review**, not “broken” based on one release or a premarket share-price move. [AMZN thesis card](AMZN_2026-09-28.md)

## Conditional action framework and catalyst timing

- **If** AWS growth translates into sustained segment profit while capex growth moderates and FCF begins to recover, the original undervaluation case gains credibility; reassess the target with updated cash-flow inputs before considering any addition.
- **If** AWS sales continue rising but AWS margins, utilization or free cash flow deteriorate despite the spending, lower the valuation multiple and risk-adjusted target; distinguish capacity timing from weak economics.
- **If** management repeatedly raises capex while failing to show a credible cash payback, or AWS profit growth stalls for multiple quarters, test the card's thesis breakers and review the position independently of the entry price.

The next evidence gates are Q4 results and guidance, AWS revenue **and** operating margin, contracted backlog conversion, ad growth, retail margins, depreciation, cash capex and TTM FCF. This is a research framework, not a leveraged-trading instruction.
