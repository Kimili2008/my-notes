### A disciplined substitution framework

If the objective is to lower common-factor risk, a reasonable research sequence is:

1. **Do not add ORCL on top of the existing AI bucket.** Consider it only as a measured replacement for part of MU/NVDA/AVGO after reviewing capex funding and FCF conversion.
2. **Compare UBER with ROAD and the space sleeve.** UBER's verified free-cash-flow profile may improve portfolio quality, but the position should remain conditional on mobility margins and Delivery profitability.
3. **Use MCD or PACS as a small stabilising sleeve.** MCD is the more defensive compounder; PACS offers more growth but materially more reimbursement and operating risk.
4. **Do not replace NU/SOFI with SEZL solely because of its growth rate.** The three names share consumer-credit sensitivity and should be sized as one risk bucket.

These comparisons should be revisited after the next earnings releases and after the actual portfolio cash balance and cost bases are available.

## Risk controls

- Keep any single early-stage or binary-catalyst name within a pre-set ceiling; the current SPCX and RKLB combined exposure is above a conservative 10–18% planning range.
- Use a thesis stop, not an arbitrary price stop: define the KPI or event that would invalidate the card before adding.
- Avoid leverage for routine volatility. The portfolio already has high beta and correlated duration; leverage would convert a manageable drawdown into a liquidity problem.
- Review actual weights after every trade and after major earnings. The present weights are quote-based estimates and exclude cash, options, taxes and unlisted assets.

## Summary

The highest-quality adjustment is a gradual reduction of common-factor concentration and a gradual increase in cash or diversified platform exposure, while preserving the strongest secular theses. No position is marked “broken” on the information reviewed; MU and RKLB are the two positions requiring the most immediate evidence before adding.
