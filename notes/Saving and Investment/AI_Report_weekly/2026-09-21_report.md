# Portfolio Weekly Research Note — 21 September 2026

## Executive Summary

**Stance: constructively cautious.** The portfolio remains positioned for the AI infrastructure and platform-profit cycle, but the macro regime has become less forgiving. The Federal Reserve raised rates by 25 basis points on 16 September—its first increase since 2023—and the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield finished the week near 5.0%. August CPI increased 0.4% month-on-month and 3.4% year-on-year. Higher real and nominal discount rates are a direct headwind to long-duration growth equities, especially where valuation already embeds multi-year AI capex growth.  

Fundamentals, however, have not broken. Broadcom delivered exceptional Q3 execution and guided Q4 revenue to approximately $34.8bn (+93% YoY), including $21.7bn of AI semiconductor revenue. Micron remains the key near-term catalyst: it reports fiscal Q4 on 30 September, with the HBM4 / AI-memory thesis now moving from expectation to an earnings-and-guidance test. Uber's early autonomous-vehicle launches in London and regulatory progress in Madrid reinforce—not prove—its strategic claim to be the demand, dispatch and operating layer in autonomous ride-hailing.

**Bottom line:** retain core exposure to cash-generative AI beneficiaries, but do not add leverage ahead of Micron earnings or into a rising-yield regime. Rebalance incremental risk away from overlapping semiconductor beta and toward liquidity / defensive compounders until the next earnings checkpoints confirm that estimates can outrun discount-rate pressure.

The practical regime change is not that AI demand has disappeared. It is that the market is increasingly differentiating between companies that can convert AI demand into near-term revenue, gross margin and free cash flow, versus those supported primarily by long-dated optionality. The portfolio should therefore be managed by **earnings revision durability**, balance-sheet resilience and valuation discipline—not by momentum alone.

## Company Research Updates and Thesis Tests

### Broadcom (AVGO) — Reinforced, but do not confuse guidance with diversification

Broadcom reported Q3 revenue of $29.6bn (+86% YoY), AI semiconductor revenue of $16.7bn (+221% YoY), and $13.7bn of free cash flow (46% of revenue). Q4 guidance calls for approximately $34.8bn of revenue and $21.7bn of AI semiconductor revenue. These figures strongly reinforce the thesis that custom accelerators, networking and optical interconnect are participating in the AI build-out alongside GPUs.

The unresolved issue is the same as the thesis card's core breaker: whether the AI revenue stream is durable and multi-customer, or concentrated in a limited set of projects. At $357.61, the share price is above the card's "$350 can add gradually" level but below its $450–520 base fair-value range. Maintain as a core position; add only on evidence of customer diversification or a valuation reset, rather than mechanically chasing the earnings print.

### Micron (MU) — Reinforced, with the largest binary near-term catalyst

Micron's last reported quarter established record revenue and cash generation; HBM4 is in high-volume shipments and HBM4E is targeted for calendar-2027 volume production. Those facts support the thesis that AI memory intensity can lift the industry's mid-cycle earnings power. The market price of $1,015.80 is close to the card's $1,000 "buy in tranches" area and below its $1,500 base fair value.

Fiscal Q4 earnings on 30 September are a portfolio-level event. The report must validate all three legs of the thesis: (1) HBM ramp / qualification and customer commitments, (2) DRAM and NAND pricing / supply discipline, and (3) a credible outlook for 2027 capacity and margins. A guidance miss or a hint that industry supply is responding faster than AI demand would be a thesis-warning, even if headline EPS remains strong. Do not use 2–4x leverage ahead of this result.

### Grab (GRAB) — Intact, but still an execution thesis rather than a valuation-only trade

At $2.795, GRAB is marginally inside the thesis card's stated strong-buy zone (≤$2.80) and below its $3.95 fair-value estimate. The valuation case remains conditional: the company must sustain roughly 20% growth through the maturity path while expanding pre-tax margin toward 15%. The critical scorecard is unchanged—delivery and mobility growth, financial-services growth, margin trajectory and credit losses. Two consecutive quarters below 20% revenue growth or margin deterioration would move the thesis from "intact" to "under review."

Treat GRAB as a capped satellite position, not as a substitute for high-quality cash-flow exposure. The discount offers optionality; it does not remove execution, regional macro or credit risk.

### Uber (UBER) — Strategic thesis reinforced; economics remain unproven

Uber and Wayve launched supervised autonomous rides in London, and Uber / WeRide / AVOMO received Spain's first national permit for Level 4 passenger-vehicle operations. These developments support the thesis that Uber can remain the demand-and-dispatch layer as autonomous fleets scale. They do **not** establish the eventual take rate, customer ownership or unit economics.

At $70.50, UBER is below the thesis card's $70–75 add band and materially below the $105–115 base fair-value range. For a confirmed holding, the risk/reward remains favorable only if core gross bookings and delivery profitability continue to compound while AV partners remain economically complementary. Keep AV economics—not simply AV launches—as the decisive KPI.

### NVDA, META and AMZN — Core quality, but thesis cards need to be formalised

These are high-quality, cash-generative platforms / infrastructure leaders, but no current thesis card is stored for them. Their concentration to the same AI capex cycle is the portfolio's principal hidden risk: AVGO, MU, NVDA and AMZN/AWS can all be impaired simultaneously by a lower hyperscaler-capex outlook or by rising discount rates. Before increasing any one of them, define a written earnings, valuation, breaker and sizing framework equal to the AVGO/MU standard.

## Risk Controls

1. **No incremental leverage** around Micron's 30 September earnings or while the 10-year yield remains around 5%; the expected volatility can dominate a correct long-term thesis.
2. **Cap overlapping AI semiconductor exposure.** AVGO + MU + NVDA should be evaluated as one factor bucket, not three independent diversification sources.
3. **Preserve liquidity.** The existing SGOV / cash concept is strategically useful: it funds genuine dislocations and reduces the probability of forced selling.
4. **Separate thesis from price action.** A one-day move is not confirmation; a change in bookings, pricing, margins, capacity discipline, customer concentration or AV economics is.
