## Investment conclusion

TSMC's Q3 report is a **positive industry read-through** for the portfolio's AI semiconductor exposure, while yesterday's broad risk-off move highlights its **correlated valuation risk**. Q3 U.S.-dollar revenue was **$46.72 billion**, above the $44.6–45.8 billion guidance range; high-performance computing represented **68%** of revenue versus 66% in Q2, with HPC revenue up 22% sequentially. Q4 revenue guidance of **$52.3–54.3 billion** implies continued strong leading-edge demand. Gross margin of 66.4% declined 1.3 percentage points sequentially, principally due to the initial N2 ramp; it remained within the prior 65–67% guide. These data support the AI infrastructure cycle but do **not** independently prove NVDA or AVGO customer orders, gross margins, or MU's HBM/DRAM pricing. [TSMC Q3 results and guidance](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/quarterly-results/2026/q3); [TSMC management report](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-10/f83780000ddc0c3b48e57a87eab6814c4ab35058/3Q26%20ManagementReport.pdf).

## Expected value and risk

No numerical fair-value or thesis-card target revision is justified from a supplier's result alone. The evidence modestly increases confidence in the *sector demand* leg of AVGO/NVDA and the AI-related demand context for MU, but does not resolve their company-specific unit economics. The opposing factor is macro: September CPI, published yesterday, showed 3.7% headline inflation year over year and 2.6% core inflation. Higher yields could compress multiples across semiconductor, software and space holdings even if operating forecasts remain sound. [BLS September CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm). The portfolio's concentration makes a factor drawdown more important than any single stock's daily move.
