## RKLB: what changed versus the written thesis

The card's 12-month base and positive cases assume Neutron's development moves toward a broadly successful 2027 commercialization after a Q4-2026 hardware/launch milestone. The company now says first flight is expected **sometime in 2027** and has not supplied a narrower launch date. This shifts expected Neutron cash flows later and increases schedule, development-cost and financing uncertainty. The old **$85–105** 12-month base target and **$78–85** risk-adjusted reference are therefore **stale pending a model update**; a lower share price alone does not make those old targets newly actionable. The card's **0/10** risk-adjusted rating should not be automatically raised because the stock fell. [RKLB thesis card](RKLB_2026-09-28_revised.md); [Rocket Lab SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819994/000181999426000067/rklb-10142026ex991.htm).

What remains unproven: whether the identified manufacturing issue is fully remedied in flight hardware, whether a 2027 launch window holds, the amount of incremental R&D/capex and the timing of customer revenue. Conversely, the delay is **not** evidence that the already-operating Space Systems and Electron/HASTE businesses have failed. The card's direct invalidation checks remain Space Systems growth below 15%, backlog not growing for two quarters, Space Systems gross margin below 30%, persistent cash deterioration, or Neutron slipping **past 2027** / failing its first flight.
