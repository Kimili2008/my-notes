## Investment conclusion

Nu Holdings (NU) closed 26 October down **16.99%**, from $15.16 to $12.59, after Brazil re-elected Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. This is a material portfolio event, but the price move alone does not establish that Nu's customer growth, unit economics, credit quality, or management execution deteriorated. The immediate change is the market's pricing of Brazilian political, currency, rates, and fiscal risk. Brazil is Nu's largest market, so those risks cannot be dismissed as irrelevant to intrinsic value.

At the 71.75 shares recorded in the current `Portfolio_AI.md`, the $2.57-per-share decline represents approximately **$184.40** of mark-to-market loss. That equals **2.17% of the $8,484.93 disclosed equity sleeve marked in the 26 October weekly report**, *not* a verified 2.17% loss for the full account: cash and any unlisted assets are unknown. The impact is large enough to warrant a thesis check without treating a one-day sell-off as a forced trade signal.

## Thesis and expected-value implications

The existing Nu card identifies Brazil concentration, BRL translation, risk-adjusted net interest margin, and delinquency as key underwriting variables. Its prior **$17–18 risk-adjusted target range** was anchored to a substantially different market and FX setup; it should be treated as **under review**, not mechanically divided by today's price to declare a larger margin of safety. The share-price fall exceeds any simple one-for-one currency explanation and likely embeds a higher country risk premium; this is an inference, not proof of a permanent earnings impairment.

Near-term expected value cannot be recalculated responsibly from the election result and one trading session alone. A revised case requires (i) the persistence of BRL weakness, (ii) the path of Selic and Nu's deposit/funding costs, (iii) whether borrower stress changes loss rates and risk-adjusted NIM, and (iv) credible information on the government's fiscal agenda. If these stay stable, the sell-off could create value; if they weaken together, the old target and position size may be too optimistic. In particular, the card's **risk-adjusted NIM below 9% for two consecutive quarters** remains a concrete thesis-invalidation checkpoint, rather than a price-based stop.

## Catalysts and conditional decision framework

**Next checks:** Brazil fiscal-policy announcements; BRL and Brazilian yield stabilization versus a second leg of stress; Nu's next reported deposits, loan growth, 15–90-day and 90+ day delinquencies, credit-loss provisions, risk-adjusted NIM and ROE. Do not infer these KPIs from the ADR tape.

- **If** currency/rates stabilize and subsequent Nu credit and funding KPIs remain inside the written thesis, retain the operating thesis and re-underwrite the valuation before considering any addition.
- **If** BRL weakness and higher funding costs persist, or credit metrics worsen, lower the base-case earnings/valuation assumptions and review the portfolio's Brazil/fintech concentration before increasing exposure.
- **If** the written invalidation conditions are met—particularly sustained risk-adjusted NIM below 9%—treat the thesis as potentially broken and reassess the position independently of the purchase price.
